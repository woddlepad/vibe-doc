+# Lessons from past consultations
+
+## Every abnormal finding must be explained by your final diagnosis
+- Before submitting, list every abnormal finding from the exam, tests and history. Your diagnosis
+  must account for all of them. If one finding does not fit, either pick a diagnosis that explains
+  it or resolve it with a targeted test. Do not just write it down as a "gap" and submit anyway.
+- Objective findings beat the patient's own account. Patients describe things in lay terms and
+  often do not notice deficits (hearing loss, weakness, visual field loss, a skin lesion, a mass).
+  If the exam or a test shows an abnormality the patient denies, believe the exam or test.
+- If there is a discrepancy, check it with the objective test (for example audiometry, visual
+  fields, a lab value) rather than asking the patient again.
+
+## Pick between closely related diagnoses using the one feature that separates them
+- Many diagnoses come in pairs that differ by a single feature. Your answer must name the specific
+  one the findings support, not the more common or more general neighbour. Examples of such
+  pairs: vestibular neuritis (vertigo only) vs labyrinthitis (vertigo plus hearing loss);
+  Ménière disease vs vestibular migraine; cellulitis vs erysipelas; Crohn disease vs ulcerative
+  colitis; nephritic vs nephrotic syndrome.
+- When you recognise such a pair, look for the separating feature on purpose (ask, examine or
+  test for it) and let it decide.
+
+## Use the record you have
+- Findings not in the record come back as "not in the record" or normal. If a test you want is
+  not available, do not keep asking for variants of it. Move on and work with the abnormal
+  findings you already have; they were put in the case for a reason.
+- Order the simple test that targets the organ involved (audiometry for ear complaints, urinalysis
+  for urinary complaints, ECG for chest pain) early. It often holds the deciding finding.
+
+## Name the diagnosis at the right level
+- Give the specific disease, not a broad category or a symptom ("labyrinthitis", not "peripheral
+  vestibulopathy"; "community-acquired pneumonia", not "lower respiratory infection").
+- Do not pack alternatives into the diagnosis field. Put one condition there and the rest in the
+  differential.
