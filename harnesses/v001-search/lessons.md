# Lessons from past consultations

- An abnormal exam finding that conflicts with the patient's denial is the key clue. Do not
  set it aside as a "discrepancy to resolve later". Build the diagnosis around it.
  Example pattern: acute vertigo after a cold. Normal hearing points to vestibular neuritis.
  Hearing loss on exam or audiometry points to labyrinthitis, even if the patient says hearing is fine.
- Many answer keys hinge on one discriminating feature between sibling diagnoses. Examples:
  neuritis vs labyrinthitis (hearing), and similar pairs in every specialty. Before submitting,
  ask: "What is the closest alternative, and which finding separates them?" Then check that
  finding.
- Don't spend several requests re-asking for exam details the record lacks. One "not in the
  record" reply means the detail is unavailable. Use the remaining budget on a different test
  that could confirm or refute your diagnosis (e.g. audiometry, a targeted lab, imaging).
